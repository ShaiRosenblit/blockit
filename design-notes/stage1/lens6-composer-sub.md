# Lens 6: Information variant — 8 candidates

Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5 (Composer substitute)
Lens: 6 (information variant — change what the player can see / when)

Each candidate modifies visibility of board state, tray, target, or piece information such that information reveal becomes a **stateful decision** — the player's actions cause new information to become visible, creating distinct questions vs. base mode.

---

## Candidate L6-1 — Revealed Frontier (target hidden; revealed cell-by-cell on placement)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Target shape is entirely hidden at game start. Board is fully visible (pre-fill visible if any).
  - When the player places a piece, any board cell 4-adjacent to the newly-placed piece cells reveals the target value for that adjacent cell (0 = empty in target, 1 = filled in target). Reveals stop at board boundary.
  - Tray always visible. Pieces draw from a finite tray sequence (puzzle-like); no infinite classic-mode tray.
  - Line clears are DISABLED (no clear mechanic to avoid confusing what "reaching target" means).
  - Goal: after all pieces placed, board state matches the hidden target exactly.
- Win condition: all pieces from tray sequence placed AND board state equals revealed-target state exactly.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted AND board ≠ revealed target, OR current tray has no legal placement.
- What's hidden: entire target grid (initially unknown).
- What reveals: each placement reveals target cells 4-adjacent to the placed piece footprint.
- New question per placement: "Where should I place this piece to reveal the *specific cells* I need to know next — cells that will unlock the correct placement for my remaining pieces?" Placement becomes a scout-and-commit action. A piece placed defensively (away from useful region) wastes reveal-power.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Placement footprint. (1) Wanted: footprint size determines reveal-width — bigger pieces reveal more target cells on the same turn. (2) Feared: large piece placed in one region locks you into committing cells there before you know what the target is elsewhere on the board, risking a dead board state.
- Antagonist: the hidden target shape itself — the player must defeat ignorance by strategic revelation.
- Negative-space constraint: cells outside the target must be left empty; a placement overhanging the target is illegal (same as Puzzle mode).
- Order coupling: revealing 5 cells on turn 1 (via big piece) determines which cells are known on turn 2, which regions you can legally reach, and which cells remain unknown going into the final placement. Different piece orderings produce different information states at each step.
- Replay test: the hidden target is fixed per instance (generator-seeded). After 10 plays of the same seed, the player memorizes the target shape. However, the *optimal piece order* for scouting vs. committing on a fresh seed is non-trivial. The mode punishes memorization across runs (each new instance is different), but is vulnerable to the *same-instance* replay becoming rote once target is known. Acceptable: the *first play* of each instance poses a genuine Exploration question; the "10th play" question is different (now "commit efficiently to match memorized target") but not as deep.
- **AUTO-REJECT: Potential flaw — replay test weakness**. After 10 plays of the same puzzle seed, the target is memorized and the revelation-based decision vanishes. The mode solves once, then becomes Classic with memorized-target. (However, if generator emits new seeds each run, this is acceptable — clarification needed on whether this is Puzzle-like finite-instance or Classic-like per-session.)

## Candidate L6-2 — Partial Board Vision (board pre-fill hidden; revealed on adjacency)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Board has a pre-seeded full state, but **pre-fill cells are face-down** (not visible) at game start.
  - Target shape is fully visible.
  - When a player places a piece, any pre-fill cells 4-adjacent to the placed piece become visible.
  - When line clears trigger, cells adjacent to the cleared area are also revealed.
  - Tray always visible (or limited as per Puzzle mode).
  - Goal: match target shape exactly.
- Win condition: board state matches target exactly at end of tray.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted AND board ≠ target, OR no legal placement available.
- What's hidden: all pre-fill cells on the board initially.
- What reveals: placement or clears adjacent to pre-fill.
- New question per placement: "Where should I place this piece to discover pre-fill regions that threaten my target? Should I clear an adjacent empty region first to reveal the block beneath, or place conservatively to avoid discovering an obstacle I can't handle?" Placement becomes defensive (avoid bad surprises) or offensive (seek information about obstacles).
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Pre-fill. (1) Wanted: pre-fill is revealed slowly, so the player can adapt and plan around it. (2) Feared: a large pre-fill region revealed too late locks the player into a failed board state (you committed to filling cells that turn out to collide with hidden obstacles).
- Antagonist: the hidden pre-fill obstacle that the player must survive by careful revelation order.
- Negative-space constraint: cells outside target must be empty; pre-fill cells outside target must also be empty (they are "obstacles" the player must learn to avoid).
- Order coupling: revealing a column of pre-fill early changes the available placements in that column later. Piece order determines which pre-fill regions are exposed and which remain hidden going into final turns.
- Replay test: the pre-fill pattern is fixed. After 10 plays of the same instance, the player has memorized the pre-fill layout. The game becomes "Classic + memorized pre-fill" — the reveal mechanic no longer triggers decisions because the obstacle is already known. Vulnerable to same-instance replay.

## Candidate L6-3 — Queued Tray (only 2 of 3 visible; 3rd revealed on commit)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Tray of 3 pieces as in Classic, but only pieces 0 and 1 are visible. Piece 2 is hidden (shown as "?").
  - When the player places piece 0, it is removed from tray; piece 1 slides to position 0, a new piece from the queue is generated and placed at position 1 (now visible), and piece 2 remains hidden at position 2.
  - When the player places the newly-visible piece 1, it is removed; piece 2 becomes visible at position 1, and a new hidden piece enters at position 2.
  - Target and board fully visible.
  - Line clears active (or as per base mode).
  - Goal: survive N trays or match target (as per chosen base mode).
- Win condition: N trays completed without lose condition (or target matched, depending on base).
- Lose condition: current tray has at least one piece, but no legal placement exists for any visible piece, OR current tray is exhausted.
- What's hidden: piece 2 (shape, size, color).
- What reveals: piece 1 after the player commits and places piece 0.
- New question per placement: "Should I place piece 0 now (it fits okay) to see what piece 2 looks like, or hold piece 0 to preserve my board state in case piece 2 is unusable?" Information-gathering creates a timing decision. Committing early reveals the obstacle; waiting risks getting locked into a board that cannot absorb piece 2.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: The tray queue. (1) Wanted: committing to piece 0 reveals piece 2, unlocking the next decision. (2) Feared: committing to piece 0 when the board is tight means piece 2 might be unplaceable and the game is lost — the player is forced to commit blind to a risky board state.
- Antagonist: the hidden piece 2 — the player must defeat uncertainty by deciding when to commit.
- Negative-space constraint: the board must have at least one legal placement for piece 1 or the current round is already lost.
- Order coupling: placing piece 0 in position (3, 3) sets the board state that must absorb both pieces 1 and piece 2. Different placement orders for the visible pieces produce different board topologies and different likelihoods that the hidden piece fits.
- Replay test: the sequence of pieces is fixed by the seed. After 10 plays of the same seed, the player memorizes the hidden piece queue. The decision "commit to see piece 2 or wait" becomes automatic because piece 2 is known. The mode collapses to a pre-memorized game. However, each new seed presents a new hidden queue, so the mode works across multiple game instances.

## Candidate L6-4 — Target Partial Reveal (target shown as 70% opacity; hidden cells must be inferred)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Generator seeds the game with a target shape and a **visible subset** of target cells. Approximately 70% of target cells are shown; the remaining 30% are hidden (not drawn on the target visualizer).
  - Pre-fill may exist.
  - When a piece placement exactly matches a hidden target cell (the placed cell touches or fills a cell corresponding to a hidden target cell), that target cell flips to visible.
  - Alternatively: a player places a piece, and if any of the piece's cells happen to cover a location in the target footprint, those target cells reveal (regardless of whether they are filled or empty in the target).
  - Goal: board state matches full target (including hidden cells) exactly.
- Win condition: board state matches full target (known + hidden cells) exactly.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted AND board ≠ full target, OR no legal placement, OR board violates known target constraints.
- What's hidden: 30% of the target cells (scattered across the board).
- What reveals: placement interaction; if the player places a piece in a location touching a hidden target cell, that cell becomes visible.
- New question per placement: "Should I place a piece in the likely-hidden region to scout it (and risk overcommitting to that region), or place conservatively in the known-target region and hope the hidden cells align?" Placement becomes a balance between scouting (probe unknown regions) and commitment (fill what you know).
- Dual-purpose mechanic: The target grid. (1) Wanted: the visible target is a guide; filling it is success. (2) Feared: the hidden target creates a risk — placing conservatively to match visible target may violate hidden cells, creating an invalid board state.
- Antagonist: the hidden target cells that the player must discover without overcommitting.
- Negative-space constraint: cells outside the target must remain empty; the player must infer the full target footprint from 70% visibility.
- Order coupling: filling a known-target region first commits the board to a shape; discovering hidden target cells later may reveal that a hidden extension was required, forcing a re-evaluation of earlier placements. Piece order determines which regions you fill first and which hidden cells you discover last.
- Replay test: the hidden target is fixed per instance. After 10 plays, memorization of the full target shape is complete. The scouting decision ("should I probe here?") becomes deterministic because the target is known. Vulnerable to same-instance replay.

## Candidate L6-5 — Piece-Color Hidden (shape visible, color hidden until placed)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Tray shows piece shapes (silhouettes) but not their colors. All pieces appear as gray outlines.
  - When a piece is placed on the board, its color is revealed (e.g., the piece becomes red, blue, green).
  - Color matters: scoring or clearing rules are color-dependent (e.g., line clears only if all cells in the line are the same color, OR same-color runs of 4+ clear).
  - Target shape is visible; colors in target cells may be fully or partially hidden.
  - Goal: match target shape AND colors exactly.
- Win condition: board state matches target (shape and color) exactly.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted AND board ≠ target, OR no legal placement.
- What's hidden: piece colors (revealed on placement).
- What reveals: placement itself.
- New question per placement: "This gray piece fits the shape; when I place it, what color will it be, and will that color help or hurt the target color pattern?" The piece becomes a "lottery" — it has a known shape but unknown color-role. Placement is the only way to resolve the uncertainty.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Color. (1) Wanted: the player wants the revealed color to match the target color for that region, advancing the goal. (2) Feared: the revealed color might be the wrong color, forcing a costly undo (if allowed) or creating a mismatched board that requires expensive rework.
- Antagonist: the hidden color assignment that may or may not align with the target.
- Negative-space constraint: cells outside target must remain empty (no role for color there).
- Order coupling: placing a piece in position A reveals a color; if that color is wrong for position A but right for position B, the player is committed to a suboptimal board. Different piece orders produce different color-reveals and different board states.
- Replay test: the color assignment is fixed by seed. After 10 plays of the same seed, the player memorizes the piece colors. The "lottery" decision disappears because colors are known. Same-instance replay becomes deterministic.

## Candidate L6-6 — Tray Reveal Chain (next tray only unlocked by winning the current tray)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Game is a sequence of tray challenges. The player is given a tray of 3 pieces and a local goal (e.g., clear a specific region, or fill a specific small target).
  - The player must complete the goal using ONLY this tray.
  - If successful, the next tray is revealed and a new local goal is presented.
  - If the player fails (tray exhausted, goal unmet), the game ends (no global win, just local sequence of wins).
  - The sequence and difficulty of trays is hidden; the player does not know how many trays remain or what the global objective is.
  - Each local goal reveals a small piece of a larger pattern (e.g., "clear row 5 in tray 1, then clear column 3 in tray 2, then fill a 3x3 region in tray 3" — and the full pattern is only visible in hindsight).
- Win condition: complete the maximum sequence of tray challenges without failure, OR reach a final tray challenge and complete it.
- Lose condition: any tray challenge fails (goal not met before tray exhaustion).
- What's hidden: future tray compositions, future local goals, the global end state, the total length of the game.
- What reveals: each successful tray challenge reveals the next tray and next goal.
- New question per placement: "Should I optimize this tray for the stated local goal (clear row 5), or should I position pieces to set up a potential future goal I can infer from earlier trays?" The player must balance immediate success (win the current tray) with speculation about hidden future goals.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: The tray sequence. (1) Wanted: completing a tray reveals the next tray, advancing the game. (2) Feared: optimizing for a guessed future goal may cause failure on the current goal, ending the game.
- Antagonist: the hidden sequence of escalating challenges.
- Negative-space constraint: each local goal constrains the board state that the player must achieve with only the visible tray.
- Order coupling: the order of local goals across trays creates dependencies. Completing tray 1 by clearing row 5 may prevent a favorable board state for tray 2's goal (fill the upper-left quadrant). The game requires the player to infer the implicit sequence and balance local vs. anticipated global wins.
- Replay test: the tray sequence is fixed by seed. After 10 plays of the same seed, the player has memorized the sequence and goals. The "should I speculate about future goals?" decision becomes deterministic because future goals are known. Same-instance replay becomes a solved puzzle. However, the mode is designed to be played once per seed (like a roguelike level), so replay is not the intended use case.
- **AUTO-REJECT: Possible design flaw — sequence memorization eliminates decision**. The inherent replay vulnerability means this mode is a single-play-per-instance puzzle, not a replayable mode. It may be acceptable if framed as a campaign or roguelike progression, but the "10th play test" expects repeated plays with fresh decisions. Clarify whether the intended use is single-play-per-seed or multi-play.

## Candidate L6-7 — Board Region Fogged (board divided into 4 quadrants; each revealed on touch)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Board is divided into four 5x5 quadrants (or 2x2 "zones" on a 10-cell board). Each quadrant is initially fogged (pre-fill cells in that quadrant are not visible).
  - When the player places a piece such that one of its cells touches (4-adjacent or overlaps) the boundary of a fogged quadrant, that entire quadrant is revealed (all pre-fill cells in it become visible).
  - Target is fully visible.
  - Line clears active.
  - Goal: match target shape exactly.
- Win condition: board state matches target exactly.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted AND board ≠ target, OR no legal placement.
- What's hidden: pre-fill cells within fogged quadrants.
- What reveals: an entire quadrant becomes visible when the player places a piece adjacent to or within its boundary.
- New question per placement: "Should I place a piece to reveal the next fogged quadrant (risking what I'll find), or should I consolidate my current board and reveal only when forced?" Placement becomes strategic about whether to probe or consolidate. Probing early reveals obstacles and gives time to adapt; probing late commits the board and may reveal an insurmountable obstacle.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: The quadrant boundaries. (1) Wanted: revealing a quadrant unlocks new placement options and information. (2) Feared: revealing a quadrant may expose a large pre-fill region that locks the board into a failed state, with no room to adapt.
- Antagonist: the hidden pre-fill regions that the player must manage by careful revelation order.
- Negative-space constraint: cells outside target must be empty; pre-fill cells outside target must also be empty (they are obstacles).
- Order coupling: revealing quadrant A in turn 1 exposes obstacles that force a specific board shape; revealing quadrant B in turn 2 may be impossible given that shape, or may unlock a new path. Piece order determines which quadrants are revealed first and in what sequence.
- Replay test: the pre-fill pattern is fixed per instance. After 10 plays, the player has memorized the four quadrant layouts. The "should I reveal now or wait?" decision becomes deterministic because the obstacles are known. Same-instance replay becomes rote.

## Candidate L6-8 — Color-Gated Zones (pieces can only be placed in zones matching their color)
- Lens: 6 (information variant)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku 4.5
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Board is pre-tinted with color regions (red zone, blue zone, green zone, yellow zone). These regions are fully visible and never move.
  - Each piece has a color. A piece can only be placed in a zone matching its color (a red piece can only be placed in the red zone).
  - Pieces and zone colors are drawn from the same palette (e.g., 4 colors, 4 zones).
  - Pre-fill may exist within zones.
  - Target is fully visible.
  - Line clears active (or disabled, depending on base mode).
  - Goal: match target shape exactly, with pieces confined to their color zones.
- Win condition: board state matches target exactly, with no piece in a zone mismatching its color.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted AND board ≠ target, OR current tray has no legal placement (all pieces are confined to their zones, and no piece's zone has a legal placement).
- What's hidden: the tray piece colors are visible, so there is no hidden information in the tray. **However, the implicit hidden information is the "reachability" of each piece — a piece of one color cannot reach a zone of another color.**
- What reveals: nothing is revealed during play; all constraints are visible from the start.
- New question per placement: "This blue piece can only go in the blue zone, but the blue zone is nearly full. Should I place the red piece (which has more room in the red zone) to clear the board and create space for the blue piece later, or should I wait and hope a future tray has smaller pieces?" The color-zone constraint creates a **partitioning problem** — the player must manage four separate sub-boards, not a unified board.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Color. (1) Wanted: color zones physically partition the board, guaranteeing each color has a designated region and can't interfere with other colors. (2) Feared: color zones create isolated regions that may not have enough space for all pieces of that color — the player can't "borrow" space from an adjacent zone because the color doesn't match.
- Antagonist: the partition itself — the player must defeat the spatial fragmentation by balancing all four zones simultaneously.
- Negative-space constraint: cells outside target must be empty within each zone; each zone must end with only its target cells filled.
- Order coupling: placing a large blue piece in the blue zone early fills that zone and forces all remaining blue pieces into tight configurations. Piece order determines which colors are committed to tight spaces early vs. late.
- Replay test: the zone layout and target are fixed per instance. After 10 plays, the board state is memorized. The "should I clear the red zone or fill the blue zone?" decision becomes deterministic because the optimal sequence is known. Same-instance replay becomes solved.
- **AUTO-REJECT: Possible flaw — not an information variant, rather a spatial constraint**. While color-gating affects visibility/reachability in a logical sense, it is not a *dynamic reveal* — all constraints are visible from the start. The player doesn't *learn* new information; they are *constrained* by static zones. This conflates "information variant" (reveal triggers decisions) with "constraint variant" (static rules change decisions). Recommend moving this to a different lens or reframing it to have dynamic reveals (e.g., "zone colors hidden until a piece enters them").

## Notes for Stage 2 Review

1. **Seed vs. Instance Vulnerability**: Several candidates (L6-1, L6-2, L6-4, L6-7) are vulnerable to same-instance replay memorization. This is acceptable if the game generates fresh seeds per-session (like Wordle), but problematic if the seed is fixed and the player plays the same instance multiple times. Clarify the intended replay model before full evaluation.

2. **Information as Decision**: Candidates L6-3 (Queued Tray) and L6-5 (Piece-Color Hidden) preserve the "10th play test" best because they create a genuine *commitment* decision — even if the piece is known on replay, the player must still choose *when* to commit. The decision is "when to trigger revelation" not "what is revealed," and that timing question remains fresh.

3. **Color-Gated Zones (L6-8)**: Not recommended for advancement. It is a spatial/partitioning constraint, not an information variant. The zone colors are fully visible from the start, so there is no dynamic reveal triggering decisions.

4. **Tray Reveal Chain (L6-6)**: Interesting design (roguelike progression with escalating challenges) but misaligned with the "10th play test" because it is inherently a single-play-per-seed experience. If the design goal is single-play, it is not a replayable *mode* in the Blockit sense. Recommend reframing or moving to a different lens.
