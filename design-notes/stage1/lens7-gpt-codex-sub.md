## Candidate L7-1 — Cylinder (wraparound columns, rows do not wrap)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is an 8×8 grid. Columns wrap: column 7 is 4-adjacent to column 0 (left/right).
  - Rows do NOT wrap: row 0 is not adjacent to row 7 (up/down).
  - Line clears: a full row clears normally. A full column clears, BUT the clear spans the wraparound — column 7 and column 0 are treated as spatially adjacent for geometry, so a vertical I-piece at (row R, cols 7-7 then 0-0) still occupies 4 cells and participates in a column-clear if the wrapped column is full.
  - Pieces place as in Classic (no orientation change due to topology). No pre-fill.
  - Survive N trays (classic mode win condition adapted).
- Win condition: survive 10 trays (30 pieces placed) without triggering lose.
- Lose condition: no piece in the current tray has a legal placement at any rotation.
- New question per placement: "This L-piece at col 7 can extend left (wrapping to col 0 in the same row), creating a new reachability geometry that doesn't exist in flat Classic. Can I exploit the wrapped column to set up a future line-clear that would be impossible on a flat board?" On a flat board, the right edge is a wall; here, it connects.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Column adjacency (via wraparound). (1) Wanted: wraparound provides a hidden placement option — a piece that would overspill col 7 can "land" by wrapping to col 0. (2) Feared: the wraparound can trap pieces — a piece you place at col 7 is now touching col 0, closing off moves you wanted to make there later.
- Antagonist: the edge. In Classic the edge is a wall; here it's a tunnel. The player must learn which edges collapse and which stay firm.
- Negative-space constraint: cells at the wraparound seam (col 7 and col 0) are competing for the same topological "neighbor" slot, so leaving both empty or both full has different geometric weight than in Classic.
- Order coupling: placing an I-piece vertically at col 7 before col 0 in different trays gives different reachability. An I-piece at col 7 rows 0-3 occupies a different topological position than the same I-piece at col 0 rows 0-3 — they're adjacent in wraparound space.
- Determinism check: wraparound is deterministic and baked into the board; no randomness. Forward simulation standard Tetris with wraparound adjacency rules. Solvability generator can compute reachable states via BFS on the cylinder topology.

## Candidate L7-2 — Torus (wraparound columns AND rows)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is an 8×8 grid. Both columns AND rows wrap: row 0 and row 7 are 4-adjacent (up/down); col 0 and col 7 are 4-adjacent (left/right).
  - Line clears: a row that is completely full clears (wrapping rows don't change the clear rule). A column that is completely full clears.
  - No pre-fill. Survive N trays.
- Win condition: survive 10 trays without lose.
- Lose condition: no legal placement in current tray.
- New question per placement: "This T-piece at row 7 can extend downward to row 0 (wrapping). Is that useful, or does it commit a cell I'll need for a row-clear setup at the far corner?" The four edges are now seams, not walls. A piece can connect to itself wrapping around the board.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Edge adjacency (via full wraparound). (1) Wanted: wrapping creates new placement options that compress the effective board. (2) Feared: wrapping can create unintended connections — placing a piece at (row 0, cols 3-5) is now 4-adjacent to pieces at (row 7, cols 3-5).
- Antagonist: the topology itself. The player's mental model of "left edge" and "right edge" as obstacles breaks. The board folds back on itself.
- Negative-space constraint: the four corners are now topologically equivalent to interior cells. Leaving a corner empty has different implications — it's reachable from all four cardinal directions on the torus.
- Order coupling: filling the bottom-right corner early on a torus wraps to the top-left, blocking plans that assumed those corners were independent. On a flat board they're far; on a torus they're neighbors.
- Determinism check: wraparound is deterministic. Forward simulation adapts Tetris adjacency to modular arithmetic on rows/cols. Solvability proof feasible via BFS on torus connectivity.

## Candidate L7-3 — Partitioned Board (8×8 split into 4 regions; line clears trigger per-region)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is 8×8. Visually (and mechanically) divided into a 2×2 grid of regions: Top-Left (4×4), Top-Right (4×4), Bottom-Left (4×4), Bottom-Right (4×4).
  - Each region is a semi-independent clear zone. A row clears ONLY if all 4 cells in that row within a single region are filled (not the whole row, just the 4-cell span of one region).
  - Example: row 2, cols 0-3 are the Top-Left region's row 2. If all 4 are filled, that region-row clears (even if row 2, cols 4-7 in Top-Right are empty).
  - No pre-fill. Survive N trays.
- Win condition: survive 10 trays without lose.
- Lose condition: no legal placement in current tray.
- New question per placement: "This 4-cell I-piece placed horizontally in Top-Left region, row 2 completes the REGION row 2. But in flat Classic, I'd need to fill 8 cells to clear a full row. Here, I only need 4 — but now I have 4 region-rows to manage, not 1." The clear rule is now partition-local. Pieces care about which region they're in.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Region boundary (dividing cells). (1) Wanted: region clears happen faster (4 cells vs 8), creating rapid feedback. (2) Feared: a piece placed across a region boundary is now "split" in clear accounting — the 2 cells in Top-Left and 2 in Top-Right contribute to two different region-rows, possibly slowing both.
- Antagonist: the region boundary. It partitions both the board and the clear mechanic.
- Negative-space constraint: cells at a region boundary (row 3/4 boundary, col 3/4 boundary) are mechanically distinct from interior cells — a cleared cell on one side of the boundary leaves the other side untouched, changing collapse dynamics.
- Order coupling: clearing Top-Left row 2 first leaves Top-Right row 2 unchanged. On a flat board, both sides would fall together. Here, they're independent, changing the shape of future boards.
- Determinism check: region partitions are static and visible. Clear detection is deterministic (4-cell rule per region). Forward simulation applies adapted clear rules. Solvability proof via BFS respecting region boundaries.

## Candidate L7-4 — Diagonal Lines (rows and columns are / and \ diagonals instead)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is still 8×8 grid cells, but "line clears" now trigger along / and \ diagonals, not rows/columns.
  - A / diagonal (from bottom-left to top-right within the 8×8) clears if all 8 cells along that diagonal are filled.
  - A \ diagonal (from top-left to bottom-right) clears if all 8 cells along it are filled.
  - Pieces place normally in grid cells. Adjacency is still 4-directional (up/down/left/right in grid space, NOT diagonal).
  - No pre-fill. Survive N trays.
- Win condition: survive 10 trays without lose.
- Lose condition: no legal placement in current tray.
- New question per placement: "This 4-cell I-piece placed vertically at col 2, rows 0-3 spans the main \ diagonal (top-left to bottom-right). Does it help me complete that diagonal, or should I place it horizontally to contribute to multiple / diagonals instead?" The clear geometry is now rotated 45 degrees.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Diagonal membership. (1) Wanted: a single piece can contribute to one or two diagonals; strategic placement maximizes contribution. (2) Feared: pieces placed along a diagonal may trigger unwanted clears that disrupt the board state you were building toward a different diagonal.
- Antagonist: the rotation of the clear geometry. The player's intuition about "filling rows" breaks; now they must think in diagonals.
- Negative-space constraint: the corners of the board (0,0), (0,7), (7,0), (7,7) each lie on only one 8-cell diagonal. The center cells lie on two. Negative space at the corners is less "flexible" for completing diagonals.
- Order coupling: completing the main \ diagonal first (top-left to bottom-right) constrains which future / diagonals remain winnable. The two diagonal families intersect, and order matters.
- Determinism check: diagonal membership is deterministic and pre-computed. Clear detection is deterministic. Forward simulation computes membership for each (row, col) pair and tracks diagonals. Solvability feasible via BFS.

## Candidate L7-5 — Slope (gravity toward a corner; pieces slide after placement)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is 8×8. After every piece placement (or every N placements), the entire board "tilts" toward a corner (e.g., top-left). All filled cells slide diagonally (or orthogonally) toward that corner as if gravity is pulling them there, stopping when they hit the edge or another filled cell.
  - Example: "tilt every 1 placement." After placing a piece, simulate gravity pulling cells toward (0, 0). Cell (row=5, col=5) with no filled neighbors at (4, 5) or (5, 4) slides to (4, 4), then (3, 3), etc., until blocked.
  - Line clears still active (full rows/cols clear in the final tilted state).
  - No pre-fill. Survive N trays.
- Win condition: survive 10 trays without lose.
- Lose condition: no legal placement in current tray (checked AFTER tilt simulation).
- New question per placement: "If I place this piece now, it will slide toward the top-left corner on the tilt. Will the resulting compacted board still have space for my next tray piece, or do I need to trigger a line-clear first to decompress?" Placement consequences ripple via mandatory tilt.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece placement (triggers cascade). (1) Wanted: tilts compact the board, creating opportunities for line-clears that would otherwise not be reachable. (2) Feared: tilts can ruin setups — a piece you placed in a strategic position slides away after tilt, breaking your plan.
- Antagonist: the tilt mechanic. The player must predict and plan around forced board reorganizations.
- Negative-space constraint: empty space in the direction of the tilt (e.g., toward top-left) cannot be "protected" — it will be filled as cells slide toward it. Empty space away from the tilt direction is more stable.
- Order coupling: placing piece A before tilt #1 gives a different board after tilt than placing piece B first. The tilt couples all placements.
- Determinism check: tilt direction and frequency are predetermined (e.g., "every 1 placement toward top-left"). Gravity simulation is deterministic. Forward simulation computes gravity step-by-step. Solvability proof via BFS with gravity rules baked in. NO RANDOMNESS.

## Candidate L7-6 — Rotating Board (board rotates every 4 placements; pieces place in static frame)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is 8×8. The board physically rotates 90° clockwise every 4 placements (fixed schedule, not random).
  - Pieces always place in the player's reference frame (top of screen = up, left = left). But the board beneath rotates, so a piece placed at (row=0, col=4) on the player's frame may end up at a different grid position after the rotation commit.
  - Example: placements 1-3 happen on Board-Rotation-0. After placement 4, the entire board rotates 90° clockwise (commit point). Placements 5-8 happen on the rotated board.
  - Line clears active and trigger in the player's frame before rotation, then the rotated board state is the input to the next 4-placement cycle.
  - No pre-fill. Survive N tray cycles (N cycles = N*4 placements).
- Win condition: survive 3 cycles (12 placements) without lose.
- Lose condition: no legal placement in current tray after rotation commit.
- New question per placement: "This piece is placement 3 of 4. After the next placement, the board rotates 90° clockwise. My setup in the bottom-left corner will become the bottom-right corner. Should I plan this placement assuming the rotation, or exploit the current orientation?" Placements must account for a predictable future rotation.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Board rotation (fixed schedule). (1) Wanted: rotation can rearrange a cluttered board, creating new space for future pieces. (2) Feared: rotation can destroy line-clear setups you were building — a nearly-full row in the left half of the board becomes a nearly-full column after rotation, ruining your plan.
- Antagonist: the rotation schedule. Deterministic but disruptive.
- Negative-space constraint: empty space aligned with the rotation axis (center region of the board) survives rotation; space at the edges warps to new positions. Negative space planning must account for rotational geometry.
- Order coupling: placements 1-4 in cycle 1 determine the board state at rotation commit. Cycle 2's placements (5-8) are applied to the rotated board. Changing the order of placements within a cycle changes the board at rotation commit, which changes the starting state for the next cycle.
- Determinism check: rotation happens at fixed placement counts (every 4 placements). No randomness. Forward simulation includes a rotation step at commits. Solvability proof via BFS with rotation states labeled by cycle and placement within cycle.

## Candidate L7-7 — Hexagonal Grid (cells arranged in hex grid, 6-adjacency instead of 4-adjacency)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is logically 8×8 cells, but mapped to a hexagonal grid layout. Each cell has 6 neighbors (up, down, upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, lower-right) instead of 4.
  - Pieces are classic tetromino shapes but placed on the hex grid. A 4-cell I-piece spans 4 adjacent hexagons in a line.
  - Line clears trigger on "rows" (aligned chains of 6 or 8 hexagons in a single line, depending on orientation). Column clears don't exist; instead, clears trigger on 6-cell or 8-cell straight-line chains in any of three hex-grid directions.
  - UI representation: hexagons are still drawn as a logical 8×8, but visually skewed to show the hex topology (offset rows visible).
  - No pre-fill. Survive N trays.
- Win condition: survive 10 trays without lose.
- Lose condition: no legal placement in current tray.
- New question per placement: "In hex geometry, this I-piece can align with 3 possible line directions (instead of 2 in square grid). Does extending the line in one direction help more than the others, or do I place to maximize contribution to multiple directions at once?" The branching factor of line-clear setups triples.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Hex adjacency (6-neighbor reachability). (1) Wanted: 6 neighbors create more placement flexibility — more adjacent cells, more ways to connect pieces. (2) Feared: 6 neighbors also mean pieces connect more easily to clutter, making the board dense faster.
- Antagonist: the hex topology itself. The player's intuition about "4 neighbors" breaks.
- Negative-space constraint: in hex geometry, the corners and edges have different neighbor counts than interior cells. Corners have only 3 neighbors on a hex torus; this affects clearability.
- Order coupling: in hex geometry, the connectivity graph is different, so the order of placements has different ripple effects than in square grid. The coupling is not directly translatable from square-grid intuition.
- Determinism check: hex grid adjacency is deterministic and baked in. Line membership is computed once. Forward simulation uses hex adjacency rules. Solvability proof via BFS on hex connectivity. NO RANDOMNESS.
- AUTO-REJECT NOTE: This candidate is borderline hand-wavy on UI feasibility. Hex grid rendering is non-trivial on a standard 2D grid display. Existing UI primitives assume square grid. High implementation cost, unclear if gains justify it.

## Candidate L7-8 — Gravity Wells (regions with different gravity directions; pieces slide locally per zone)
- Lens: 7 (topological twist)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (GPT-codex substitute)
- Rule changes:
  - Board is 8×8, partitioned into 4 quadrants (2×2 regions, each 4×4 cells). Each quadrant has its own gravity direction: Top-Left pulls toward (0, 0); Top-Right pulls toward (0, 7); Bottom-Left pulls toward (7, 0); Bottom-Right pulls toward (7, 7).
  - After every placement (or every N placements), pieces in each quadrant slide toward their quadrant's corner until blocked.
  - Pieces placed at region boundaries slide toward the gravity well of the region they are primarily in (or the region containing their center of mass).
  - Line clears active on the final settled board.
  - No pre-fill. Survive N trays.
- Win condition: survive 10 trays without lose.
- Lose condition: no legal placement in current tray (checked after gravity settle).
- New question per placement: "This T-piece placed at the Top-Left and Top-Right boundary will slide toward (0, 0) in the left quadrant but toward (0, 7) in the right quadrant. Where does most of its mass end up, and does it help or hurt my line-clear plans in both quadrants?" Pieces at boundaries have split consequences.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Gravity direction (per quadrant). (1) Wanted: gravity wells compact clutter, opening space in the center. (2) Feared: the four independent gravity directions can misalign pieces you wanted to keep adjacent — a piece in Top-Left that slides to (0, 0) is now far from a piece in Top-Right that slides to (0, 7).
- Antagonist: the four competing gravity wells. The board is fractured into four zones with opposed pulling forces.
- Negative-space constraint: empty space at a quadrant boundary is unstable — pieces slide away from it in opposite directions. Empty space at a corner (which is a gravity well) is stable and filled quickly.
- Order coupling: placing a piece at a boundary before gravity settles gives a different final board than placing it after. Pieces placed in different cycles settle to different well positions.
- Determinism check: gravity directions are fixed per quadrant. Gravity settle is deterministic. Forward simulation applies quadrant-local gravity after each placement. Solvability proof via BFS with 4-gravity states. NO RANDOMNESS.
