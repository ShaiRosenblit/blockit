# Lens 3 — Couple two currently-independent decisions

Author: Claude Opus
Lens: each candidate must couple TWO of {WHICH piece, ROTATION, POSITION} so the choice of one meaningfully constrains/modifies the other, leaving BOTH non-trivial. Forbidden failure: one decision becomes a deterministic function of the other (Mirror disease).

Anti-duplication: not classic, puzzle, chroma, gravity, drop, mirror, breathe, pipeline, scar, monolith.

---

## Candidate L3-1 — Tally
- Lens: 3 (couple two decisions) — couples ROTATION and POSITION.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - Each board cell carries a small integer tag in `{0,1,2,3}` painted at generation time (visible as a tiny corner glyph). Call it `tag(r,c)`.
  - When a piece is dropped, let `R = (rotation count mod 4)` and let `cells = piece footprint`. The placement is LEGAL only if `(sum over piece cells of tag(r,c)) mod 4 === R`. Otherwise the drop is rejected (piece bounces back to tray) — same UX as a current illegal drop.
  - All other rules (line clears, tray refill on empty) unchanged.
- Win condition: after N pieces (matches difficulty piece-budget), board has cleared K lines. (Endless score variant for ≥ Normal.)
- Lose condition: tray non-empty AND no legal `(piece, rotation, position)` triple exists for any tray slot.
- New question per placement: "What rotation has a footprint whose tag-sum hits the residue I need at the spot I want — or what spot's tag-sum matches a rotation I can live with?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: rotation. Normally rotation is just shape orientation; here it is ALSO a residue-class commitment that gates which positions are legal. A rotation you "want" for shape may be the rotation you "fear" because it locks you out of the open region.
- Antagonist: the tag field — a static modular constraint that rejects naive drops.
- Negative-space constraint: empty regions whose tag-sum residue doesn't line up with any rotation of any tray piece are dead zones; you must keep alive at least one (rotation, region) pair per tray slot.
- Order coupling: line clears reset cell tags to 0 in cleared lines, so order of placements changes which residues remain accessible later.
- Coupling check: ROTATION ↔ POSITION. Both stay non-trivial — for any chosen position there are typically 1–2 legal rotations (not always one), and for any chosen rotation a non-empty but pruned set of positions remains. Joint optimum (good shape AND good clear) is strictly harder than either alone. Not Mirror because rotation is not a function of position; it's a constraint on a 4-valued congruence both contribute to.

## Candidate L3-2 — Twin Bond
- Lens: 3 — couples WHICH piece and POSITION across two consecutive placements.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - Tray has 3 slots as in classic. Generator marks exactly 2 of the 3 as a "bonded pair" each refill (visible: shared color halo on the two slots).
  - Placement of a NON-bonded slot acts like classic.
  - Placement of a BONDED slot puts the piece on board normally AND arms the bond. The next placement MUST be the bond-partner. While armed, the partner's footprint must share at least one edge-adjacent cell with the just-placed bonded piece's footprint (touch-rule). If no legal touching placement exists for the partner, the bond breaks and a `scar` cell is placed where the bonded piece's centroid sat (one-time penalty per broken bond, capped at lose-trigger).
  - All other rules (line clears, refill when tray empty) unchanged. Refill picks a new bonded pair.
- Win condition: endless score; tier targets per difficulty (best-score persistence as in classic).
- Lose condition: classical no-fits, OR ≥3 broken bonds in a single refill cycle.
- New question per placement: "Where can I place piece A so that piece B still has a legal contiguous touch site that doesn't ruin my clear plan?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: piece adjacency. Normally adjacency is incidental (it's just where pieces happen to land); here adjacency is BOTH the way you build clears AND the way the bond resolves. The same primitive (an adjacent open region) is wanted for the partner's footprint and feared because it's now reserved.
- Antagonist: the bond timer — an arming state that punishes if the partner can't be adjacent.
- Negative-space constraint: you must reserve a connected pocket of the partner's exact footprint adjacent to A; that pocket is forbidden to fill with C.
- Order coupling: which of the bonded pair you place first determines which adjacent pocket must be preserved, often leading to different reachable boards.
- Coupling check: WHICH ↔ POSITION. Choosing piece A first commits to a position-shape that constrains piece B's legal positions (but leaves multiple). Choosing piece B first does the same in reverse. Neither side is forced — both retain meaningful position choices, and the optimization (rotations of both, two positions, ordering) is non-trivial. Not Mirror because the partner's exact placement is not determined; only "must touch A" is forced.

## Candidate L3-3 — Slot Lens
- Lens: 3 — couples WHICH piece (tray-slot index) and ROTATION.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - Tray slots 0/1/2 each apply a deterministic ROTATION OFFSET to the piece they hold: slot 0 = +0, slot 1 = +1 (90° CW), slot 2 = +2 (180°). The offset is added to the player's chosen rotation count when the piece is placed; the displayed preview already includes the offset, so what you see is what you get.
  - Player can still rotate freely (4 orientations reachable per slot) — the offset just shifts the cycle's starting phase.
  - When a piece is consumed, remaining tray pieces SLIDE LEFT to fill (not stay in place). Therefore the piece in slot 1 today may end up in slot 0 tomorrow with a different starting orientation phase.
  - All other rules unchanged.
- Win condition: endless score (or per-difficulty targets).
- Lose condition: classical no-fits.
- New question per placement: "Should I place piece X NOW (at the orientation phase its current slot grants) or place piece Y first and reach piece X with a different starting phase that saves me a tap?"

  (This is meaningful because tap-economy is real, but the deeper question is: ordering changes the rotation phase landscape across the whole tray, and a phase that wastes your good piece is a phase you place around.)
- Dual-purpose mechanic: slot index. Normally just a UI position; here also a rotation-phase modifier. A slot you want (because it grants the right phase for piece X) is also feared because consuming a different slot first will demote/promote piece X into a worse phase.
- Antagonist: the slide-left rule — it punishes naive piece-order choice.
- Negative-space constraint: rotations that are CHEAP this turn (already aligned by slot) are different from the rotations that are CHEAP next turn; you must keep at least one cheap-phase placement plan alive.
- Order coupling: order of consuming tray slots permutes future rotation phases — different orders yield different tap-cost frontiers and different on-board orientations if the player commits to "no manual rotate" plays.
- Coupling check: WHICH ↔ ROTATION. Player still picks any rotation manually, so rotation is not forced by slot. But the slot-induced starting phase changes the COST (taps, planning) of reaching a target rotation, and the slide-left rule means slot identity is consequential across turns. Both decisions remain open; their interaction creates a new question. Risk: if "free rotation" is too cheap, the slot effect is cosmetic — must be tuned (e.g., one rotation per tap, with a soft tap-budget, or: rotation is free but the slot's offset determines the GHOST PREVIEW shape until you commit, biasing perception).

  AUTO-REJECT-RISK: borderline. Pipeline-ish if rotation taps are de facto free — coupling would degrade to flavor. Recorded; needs a per-turn rotation cap or tap-budget to bite.

## Candidate L3-4 — Axis Vow
- Lens: 3 — couples ROTATION and POSITION via a player-chosen axis.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - At the start of each "round" (every 5 placements, or after every clear — pick: every clear, simpler), the next piece's placement establishes an AXIS: the row OR column index of the piece's anchor cell becomes the active axis, and whether it is a row-axis or column-axis is determined by the piece's chosen ROTATION parity (even rotations → row-axis at anchor row; odd rotations → column-axis at anchor col).
  - For the rest of the round, every subsequent placement must have its centroid (rounded) on the SAME axis line OR within Manhattan distance ≤ 2 of it. Pieces whose footprint cannot satisfy that are illegal.
  - End of round resets the axis. Line clears proceed as classic.
- Win condition: endless score with per-difficulty targets.
- Lose condition: classical no-fits, OR axis cannot be established (the very first piece of a round has no legal anchor — generator must prevent this).
- New question per placement: "Does this rotation declare the axis I'll be forced to live near for the next 4 placements, and does this position commit me to a line that the rest of the tray can serve?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: rotation parity. It's normally orientation; here it ALSO selects axis-orientation (row vs column) for the round. The same rotate-tap that fixes shape is the one that names the geometry of the next 4 turns.
- Antagonist: the active axis — a self-imposed corridor that the player must satisfy.
- Negative-space constraint: cells far from the axis are temporarily forbidden — wide regions of the board sit dormant per round, which forces clearing along the axis.
- Order coupling: which piece is placed FIRST in the round (the axis-setter) reshapes the legal region for the rest of the round; same tray, different first picks, very different boards.
- Coupling check: ROTATION ↔ POSITION. The first placement of a round couples them by definition: rotation parity picks axis kind, position picks axis index. Both remain non-trivial — many (rotation, position) pairs declare different axes; the player must choose one. Subsequent placements re-couple weakly via the "≤2 from axis" rule. Not Mirror because the axis is a genuine commitment, not a derived value: a different rotation OR a different position both change the axis.

## Candidate L3-5 — Footprint Echo
- Lens: 3 — couples WHICH piece and POSITION via a memory of the previous placement.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - Persistent state: `prevFootprint` = the set of cells filled by the last placed piece (cleared whenever a clear event occurs, AND on first placement of game).
  - The next placement must overlap (share ≥1 cell) the BOUNDING BOX of `prevFootprint` OR be edge-adjacent to it. Pieces that cannot satisfy are illegal.
  - When `prevFootprint` is empty (just cleared), free placement is legal and resets the chain; the resulting placement defines the new `prevFootprint`.
  - All other rules unchanged.
- Win condition: endless score / per-difficulty target.
- Lose condition: classical no-fits, no special trigger.
- New question per placement: "Which piece can advance the chain NOW into a region where the next tray piece will still fit the new bounding box?" — i.e., piece-chain planning, where shape determines reach.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: bounding box. The just-placed piece's bbox is BOTH the anchor you depend on (your only legal region) AND the constraint you'll be stuck with next turn — same primitive doing both jobs depending on whether you're upstream or downstream of it.
- Antagonist: the chain — a self-tightening trap if you place small/contracting pieces.
- Negative-space constraint: every cell outside `bbox(prev) ∪ neighbors` is forbidden until a clear resets the chain — this creates a strong incentive to time clears.
- Order coupling: order of pieces is a route through the board — same 3 pieces in different orders carve different reachable regions.
- Coupling check: WHICH ↔ POSITION. The choice of which piece (small vs wide) changes which next-step positions are reachable; the choice of position changes which piece can be played next. Both remain meaningful — usually 2–3 tray pieces have legal placements at multiple positions, and the optimization is over (this-piece, this-position, next-piece reachability). Not Mirror because position is not forced by piece choice; the bbox is a region, not a point.

## Candidate L3-6 — Quartet Cap
- Lens: 3 — couples ROTATION and POSITION via per-row rotation budgets.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - Each board row has a per-row "rotation budget" counter `rotBudget[r] ∈ {0,1,2,3}`, painted in row gutter UI (tiny digit). All rows start at 3 at game/clear-row time.
  - When a piece is placed, it CONSUMES `min(rotBudget[r])` over its occupied rows: i.e., for every row r the piece occupies, `rotBudget[r] -= rotationCount` where rotationCount ∈ {0,1,2,3}. If any occupied row would go negative, the placement is illegal.
  - When a row clears, that row's budget resets to 3. Rows are NEVER replenished otherwise.
  - All other rules (tray refill, line clears) unchanged.
- Win condition: endless score / per-difficulty target.
- Lose condition: classical no-fits.
- New question per placement: "Should I burn rotation on this piece (better shape now, fewer rotations available in those rows for future pieces) or play it under-rotated and pay later?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: rotation count. It is BOTH a shape selector AND a row-budget expenditure. A 3-rotation piece you want for shape is the same 3-rotation piece that nukes the budget of every row it touches.
- Antagonist: the row-budget field — a finite shared resource the player and the puzzle both compete over.
- Negative-space constraint: rows with budget 0 are rotation-locked (can only accept rotation-0 placements); large parts of the board can become "0-rotation only" zones the player must funnel pieces into.
- Order coupling: the order of placements determines which rows are bankrupted first, which determines whether a future piece even has legal placements.
- Coupling check: ROTATION ↔ POSITION. Rotation is freely chosen, position is freely chosen, but the joint legality is constrained by `rotBudget[r] ≥ rotationCount` over occupied rows. Both decisions retain real freedom — many (rotation, position) pairs are legal at any time — but the joint cost is non-additive. Not Mirror because rotation isn't determined by position; the player chooses the trade.

## Candidate L3-7 — Color Pact
- Lens: 3 — couples WHICH piece and POSITION via a color-zone field.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - Board is divided at generation into 3 connected color zones (visible as faint cell tints). Tray pieces have their color (same palette) — call it `pcolor`.
  - A piece may be placed ONLY if at least 50% of its footprint cells lie in its color zone (i.e., `|cells ∩ zone(pcolor)| / |cells| ≥ 0.5`).
  - Line clears: when a row/col clears, the zone field is repainted on cleared cells using the dominant color among the cleared piece-fragments (deterministic; no RNG at runtime).
  - All other rules unchanged.
- Win condition: endless score / per-difficulty target.
- Lose condition: classical no-fits.
- New question per placement: "Where in this piece's home zone can I place it such that the OTHER tray pieces still have ≥50% of THEIR home zone open?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: zone painting. A zone is BOTH a place where a same-color piece must go (resource for it) AND a place where other-color pieces are restricted (obstacle for them). Same primitive, opposite effect by piece color.
- Antagonist: the zone field shrinking under play — as your home zones fill, you starve.
- Negative-space constraint: the ≤50% rule means a piece that COULD physically fit straddling two zones is illegal — empty cells in the wrong zone don't help you.
- Order coupling: order of placements changes which zones get covered first, which (via the clear-repaint rule) changes the zone topology after a clear.
- Coupling check: WHICH ↔ POSITION. The piece's color narrows the legal region (its zone) but does NOT determine position — many positions remain in zone, and many pieces remain playable at any given position. Both decisions stay non-trivial; the joint optimum is "place the piece whose home zone is fullest first to free space, while preserving the others."

  Risk: this is adjacent to chroma — anti-duplication needed. Distinguishing feature: chroma's rule is about ADJACENT-COLOR placements; here it's about color-zone-of-board, a static field with a 50% rule. If the registry treats this as too-close to chroma, this candidate folds.

  AUTO-REJECT-RISK: marginal duplication concern with chroma. Recorded.

## Candidate L3-8 — Pivot
- Lens: 3 — couples ROTATION and POSITION via a board-fixed pivot point.
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes:
  - One cell on the board is marked the PIVOT (visible: small ring). Pivot is set at generation; pivot moves to the most recently CLEARED cell (deterministic: highest-index cleared cell of the most recent clear event).
  - When placing a piece, the player picks a position; the piece is then automatically rotated to the rotation count `R` such that the piece's anchor-to-pivot vector falls in a specific quadrant — but the player ALSO chooses one of TWO valid R values that satisfy the quadrant rule (typically R and R+2). So rotation collapses to a 2-choice given position.
  - Wait — that fails Mirror disease. Reframe:
  - Reframe: when placing a piece, the player picks a rotation R ∈ {0,1,2,3} freely. The placement is legal only if the piece's footprint INCLUDES at least one cell whose Chebyshev distance to the pivot equals `R` (i.e., distance-1 ring for R=1, etc.). R=0 means the piece must cover the pivot itself.
  - All other rules unchanged.
- Win condition: endless score / per-difficulty target.
- Lose condition: classical no-fits.
- New question per placement: "Which (rotation, position) pair lands the piece on the pivot's R-th ring while still serving my clear plan, AND does my plan tolerate the pivot moving to where my next clear lands?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: rotation count. It's a shape orientation AND a target-ring radius from the pivot. A rotation good for shape may be a rotation whose ring is fully blocked.
- Antagonist: the pivot — a moving constraint center that the player must orbit.
- Negative-space constraint: cells whose distance from pivot is not in `{0,1,2,3}` are unreachable this turn for any rotation choice — usually trivially satisfied on an 8×8 board, but corners often have only one matching ring, biting on long games.
- Order coupling: order of placements determines which clear happens first, which determines the pivot's next position, which determines next-turn legality.
- Coupling check: ROTATION ↔ POSITION. The ring-distance rule means rotation R restricts position (must touch ring R), and position narrows which R are legal (typically 2 of 4). Both stay non-trivial: usually 2–3 (rotation, position) pairs are legal per piece. The player faces a real trade: a rotation that fits your clear plan may force a position that wastes the piece. Not Mirror because rotation does not determine position uniquely; it picks a ring, and many positions land on that ring.
