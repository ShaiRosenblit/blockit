# Lens 1: Repurpose an existing primitive — 8 candidates

Generated by: Claude Opus
Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)

Each candidate repurposes a primitive (line clears, rotation, color, tray, board, or piece geometry) to do something other than its default Classic-mode job.

## Candidate L1-1 — Magnet (color repurposed: from cosmetic to placement-legal predicate, must-touch inversion)
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Board pre-filled with N "anchor" cells, each a specific palette color (visible).
  - A piece may only be placed if at least one of its cells is 4-adjacent to an existing cell of the SAME color (anchor or previously-placed piece).
  - First placement of each color is exempt only if no anchor of that color exists.
  - Line clears DISABLED. Pieces accumulate.
  - Tray of 3 as in Classic; refill on empty.
  - Generator emits a finite sequence of trays (e.g., 8 trays = 24 pieces) proven to exactly tile a target shape.
- Win condition: every cell of the target shape (encoded as pre-fill outline) is filled, and no cell outside the target is filled.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted before win, OR no piece in current tray has a legal placement.
- New question per placement: "Which anchor or already-placed cluster of my color do I extend, and which extension preserves a legal frontier for the colors still in my tray?" Color is no longer a paint job — it gates legality and partitions the board into reachable regions.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Color. (1) Wanted: the player wants their color to have many adjacent legal cells so future same-color pieces have placement options. (2) Feared: the player fears their color's frontier getting boxed in by other colors, since they cannot jump to a disconnected region.
- Antagonist: the disconnected-region trap. The player must defeat the topology of the seven color graphs simultaneously — each color is its own growing connected component competing for shared border cells.
- Negative-space constraint: cells outside the target shape must remain empty; placing a piece overhanging the target = illegal placement (so only legal positions are tray-relevant). Also: a cell adjacent to NO same-color cell is implicitly forbidden for that piece.
- Order coupling: placing red early near the green anchor blocks green's future expansion through that border. Same three pieces in different orders produce different reachable frontiers and different legal-placement sets for later trays.

## Candidate L1-2 — Pivot Budget (rotation repurposed: from free pre-action to depleting resource priced by piece size)
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Player starts with a rotation budget B (e.g., 12 rotation-cells).
  - Rotating a piece by 90 degrees costs `cells(piece)` from the budget. Rotating a 4-cell L costs 4; rotating a 1-cell mono costs 1; a 9-cell square costs 9.
  - Budget refunds +1 per line clear (line clears still active as in Classic).
  - If budget < cells(piece), that piece cannot be rotated this turn — it must be placed in its as-dealt orientation or skipped (skipping = lose if no other piece fits).
  - Goal: survive N trays without lose, OR achieve target score.
- Win condition: survive N=10 trays (30 pieces placed) without triggering lose condition.
- Lose condition: current tray has at least one piece, but no piece in the tray has a legal placement at any orientation that the player can afford given current budget.
- New question per placement: "Is this 7-cell piece worth 7 rotation budget right now, or should I place it as-dealt and save rotations for a later geometric squeeze?" Rotation becomes a cost-benefit triage, not a free pre-action.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Rotation budget. (1) Wanted: rotations open placement options on tight boards. (2) Feared: spending rotations on a big piece to fix a small problem leaves you unable to rotate the next big piece, which is exactly when you'll need it.
- Antagonist: the tray RNG (mediated by a generator that proves solvability under the budget). The board fills against you; rotations are the bullets and big pieces eat the magazine.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells you "save for later" must be reachable by an as-dealt orientation of some affordable future piece — otherwise the negative space is a trap.
- Order coupling: rotating piece A in tray 1 changes which orientations of pieces B and C in tray 3 you can afford. Same three trays in different placement orders produce different surviving budgets and different legal future moves.

## Candidate L1-3 — Chord (line clears repurposed: from "row OR column" to "row AND column on same turn")
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Filling a row alone clears NOTHING. Filling a column alone clears NOTHING.
  - A single placement that simultaneously completes at least one row AND at least one column triggers a "chord clear": both the row and column are wiped.
  - Multiple chords on one placement stack (place a piece that fills row 3, row 5, col 2, col 7 simultaneously: clear all four).
  - Pre-fill present on board. Survive N trays without filling the board.
- Win condition: complete all 3 pieces of tray N (e.g., N=8) without triggering lose.
- Lose condition: any cell becomes occupied AND no piece in the current tray has a legal placement.
- New question per placement: "Can I set up a future chord, or am I forced to half-fill a row that I now have to wait to pair with a column?" Half-completed lines now actively threaten lose because they don't self-clear.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Line completion. (1) Wanted: completed row+col together clear large area. (2) Feared: completing a row alone leaves it stuck (the row is now full and will not clear unless you also complete a passing column on the SAME placement), so it locks future piece geometry.
- Antagonist: the orphan line. Every completed-but-unchorded row/col is a permanent stripe of dead cells until you thread a column through it on a single placement.
- Negative-space constraint: cells along potential chord-pair intersections must be left empty until the right piece arrives. If you fill the intersection cell first, you can never chord that pair.
- Order coupling: filling row 3 in tray 1 means tray 3's column-completing piece must thread row 3 — but if row 3 is already full, the threading cell is occupied. Order changes which chord pairs remain reachable.

## Candidate L1-4 — Slot Modes (tray repurposed: from 3 equivalent slots to 3 distinct placement modifiers)
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Tray slots are no longer interchangeable. Slot 0 = "Stamp" (places normally). Slot 1 = "Erase" (placement removes any pre-existing cells under the piece footprint instead of filling them; if footprint hits empty cells, it's an illegal placement). Slot 2 = "Carve" (places normally, but every cell 4-adjacent to the piece footprint that is currently filled becomes empty after placement).
  - Refill: when all 3 slots empty, generator emits a new triple, again one of each modifier (modifier identity is tied to slot index, not piece identity).
  - Pre-fill on board encodes a target shape to KEEP. Goal: end state matches target.
- Win condition: board state exactly equals target pattern (specific filled-cell set) at end of tray N.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted and board != target, OR current tray has no legal play in any slot.
- New question per placement: "Do I want to add cells (slot 0), delete a contiguous block (slot 1), or trim the perimeter (slot 2)? And which of the three pieces in this tray should I assign to each verb? Wait — I can't assign; the piece IS in slot 1 by birth." (Realization: the piece's slot determines its verb, so the question is which slot's piece I use first to set up the next.)
- Dual-purpose mechanic: The tray slot. (1) Wanted: slot 1's erase verb is the only way to delete pre-fill (no line clears here); slot 2's carve trims excess. (2) Feared: slot 1 cannot be played on empty space — if you don't preserve some filled region for slot 1 to land on, slot 1 becomes a dead piece and you lose.
- Antagonist: the pre-fill pattern that must be sculpted (added to AND subtracted from) into the target — neither pure addition nor pure subtraction wins.
- Negative-space constraint: slot 1 must land entirely on filled cells; slot 2 must have at least one filled neighbor cell to do meaningful work; both rules constrain WHERE empty regions are allowed to develop.
- Order coupling: playing slot 2 (carve) before slot 1 (erase) deletes neighbors that slot 1 needed to land on. Same three pieces in different orders produce different reachable end states.

## Candidate L1-5 — Color Chord Clear (line clears repurposed: trigger is "k same-color cells in a row" instead of "row full")
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Rows and columns no longer clear when full. Instead: any time a single row or column contains 4+ contiguous cells of the same color, those contiguous cells (only those, not the whole line) clear.
  - Pieces have colors as in Classic; piece occupies cells of its color.
  - Pre-fill cells are colored, possibly mixed.
  - Goal: clear the board (remove all pre-fill) within N trays.
- Win condition: zero filled cells remain on board (entire pre-fill cleared via color-runs) at any point before tray exhaustion.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted with pre-fill remaining, OR current tray has no legal placement (overflows board edge or overlaps).
- New question per placement: "Can I extend the existing red run to length 4 by placing this red 2-cell piece adjacent to the 2-red pre-fill stripe? If I place it one cell off, I split the run into two 3-runs that never trigger." The clear key is now color-contiguity, not row-fullness.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Color. (1) Wanted: matching color to existing run-of-3 to trigger a clear. (2) Feared: laying your color next to a different color of length 3 — you've now blocked their extension toward a clear AND committed your color to a position that may not have its own run-of-3 nearby.
- Antagonist: the multi-colored pre-fill that fragments into runs of 1, 2, or 3 — each one needing the right-color piece to push it to 4 before the board clogs.
- Negative-space constraint: a row/col with mixed colors can never clear via this rule unless one color reaches 4-contiguous; gaps and wrong-color cells inside a potential run are dead permanently unless physically separated by a clear elsewhere.
- Order coupling: clearing the red run on row 3 first opens cells that the green run on column 5 needed for its 4th cell. Order of color-clears changes which subsequent clears are reachable.

## Candidate L1-6 — Board Spin (rotation repurposed: rotates the BOARD 90 degrees, not the piece)
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - The rotate gesture, when invoked, rotates the entire 8x8 board 90 degrees clockwise (all filled cells, including pre-fill, rotate with it). Pieces in tray do NOT rotate; they are placed in their dealt orientation only.
  - Each rotation costs 1 from a global "spin budget" S (e.g., S=4 spins per puzzle).
  - Line clears active.
  - Goal: clear pre-fill outline using line clears, similar to Puzzle mode, but pieces are orientation-locked.
- Win condition: pre-fill cells (a marked target set) all become empty via line clears, within tray N.
- Lose condition: spin budget exhausted AND no remaining tray piece has a legal placement, OR tray exhausted with pre-fill remaining.
- New question per placement: "If I spin the board now, that vertical I-piece becomes a horizontal threat to row 4 — but my next piece in tray, an L, gets reflected onto pre-fill I wanted to keep. Spin before or after this placement?" Rotation no longer mutates the tool; it mutates the workspace.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Rotation gesture (board spin). (1) Wanted: spin to align an as-dealt orientation with an awkward gap. (2) Feared: spinning rearranges your existing partial fills, possibly completing an unwanted line or breaking a setup you'd built.
- Antagonist: the orientation-locked tray. Pieces arrive in fixed orientations; the board is the only rotatable surface; spin budget is finite.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells "saved" for a future piece must remain empty AFTER any planned spin — a saved L-pocket in the corner becomes a different corner after one spin.
- Order coupling: spinning before placing piece A vs after produces different boards. Three placements with k spins interleaved give 3*(k+1) ordering variants, each a different end state.

## Candidate L1-7 — Tetromino Tax (piece geometry repurposed: cell count gates an action, not just space)
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - Line clears active, but with a price: when a row or column completes, the player must immediately "pay" by removing one piece from the current tray, undealt. The chosen tray-piece is sacrificed (not placed). The sacrificed piece must have cell-count >= 1; if no tray pieces remain, the clear still happens but no tax — however no clear can trigger if the placement itself emptied the tray.
  - The clear refunds: +cells(sacrificed) to a "score" (or just for win-counter).
  - Pre-fill present (Puzzle-style target).
  - Goal: clear all pre-fill.
- Win condition: all pre-fill cells emptied (via line clears).
- Lose condition: tray exhausted with pre-fill remaining, OR no legal placement available.
- New question per placement: "If I trigger the row-4 clear with this placement, I must sacrifice one of my remaining 2 tray pieces. Which one is the LEAST useful for the rest of this puzzle? Or do I avoid clearing now and lay this piece dead-cell so I can clear later when the tray refills?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece cell-count / piece presence in tray. (1) Wanted: pieces are the verb for clearing. (2) Feared: triggering a clear costs you a piece — sometimes the perfect setup piece for the NEXT clear is the one you'd be forced to sacrifice now.
- Antagonist: the pre-fill (must be cleared) AND the tax (each clear costs a future tool). Two pressures, opposed.
- Negative-space constraint: cells you leave empty for "next clear" must be reachable by tray pieces that survive the upcoming sacrifice — leave too many empties and your remaining tray after sacrifice can't fill them.
- Order coupling: clearing row 4 first (sacrifice the L) means your remaining tray for row 7 is square+T, so row 7 must be set up differently. Reverse the order and sacrifice changes — different end states.

## Candidate L1-8 — Color Key (color repurposed: as the unlock token for line clears)
- Lens: 1 (repurpose existing primitive)
- Generated by: Claude Opus
- Rule changes vs Classic:
  - A row or column being full no longer clears. Instead, it clears ONLY if all of its 8 cells are the same color.
  - Pieces colored as in Classic. Pre-fill colored.
  - Pre-fill present (Puzzle-style target). Goal: empty all pre-fill via monochrome line clears.
- Win condition: all pre-fill cells emptied.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted with pre-fill remaining, OR no legal placement.
- New question per placement: "Row 3 already has 5 red pre-fill cells and 2 empty. I have a red 2-cell piece — but if I place it horizontally there, the remaining empty cell needs a red 1-cell piece, which is not in my tray. Do I commit red here and hope, or place the red elsewhere and try a different row?"
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Color. (1) Wanted: matching the dominant pre-fill color of a near-complete line to monochromatically complete it for a clear. (2) Feared: placing the wrong color in a near-monochrome line permanently spoils that line — even if completed later, mixed colors will not clear.
- Antagonist: pre-fill arranged as ALMOST-monochrome lines, each a few cells short of clearing, each spoilable by a single off-color placement.
- Negative-space constraint: cells along an almost-monochrome line must remain empty until the matching-color piece is available; placing any other color in that line kills it.
- Order coupling: filling the red row first uses up your only red 4-piece; the green column that needed that 4-piece must now be completed differently or not at all. Reordering changes which lines are still completable.
