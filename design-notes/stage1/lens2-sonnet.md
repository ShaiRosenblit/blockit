## Candidate L2-1 — Plague

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - Board is pre-seeded with K "infected" cells (rendered with a distinct color, e.g. `'#8b2fc9'`).
  - After every placement, each infected cell that has at least 2 filled orthogonal neighbors spawns one new infected cell into a random adjacent empty cell. Spawn target is chosen deterministically by seeded RNG (seed = puzzle seed + move count), making behavior learnable.
  - An infected cell is DEFEATED (converted to a neutral filled cell of the placing piece's color) when it becomes part of a completed row or column that is cleared.
  - Pieces placed adjacent to infected cells can be used to set up the clearing line; they do not remove infection on contact alone.
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND zero infected cells remain on the board at round end.
- Lose condition: infected cell count reaches K+T (a per-difficulty threshold, e.g. 10) at any point, OR the tray is exhausted with infected cells still on the board.
- New question per placement: "Does placing here trigger a clear that removes this infected cell, OR does it give this infected cell a second filled neighbor — causing it to spread before I can clear the row?" The spread threshold (2 neighbors) means every cell adjacent to infection is a dual-purpose choice: the same fill that sets up a future line-clear may simultaneously push infection over its spreading threshold right now.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: filling cells adjacent to an infected cell simultaneously builds toward the line-clear needed to defeat it AND risks triggering its spread (the same fill does both).
- Antagonist: infected cells — they occupy board positions, spread deterministically when surrounded, and are eliminated by line clears that pass through them.
- Negative-space constraint: leaving an infected cell's row/column partially empty (to avoid triggering spread) must be balanced against needing that row/column to eventually be filled for a clear. Gaps the player deliberately leaves to starve spread also prevent clearing.
- Order coupling: placing pieces in an order that fills an infected cell's row to 7/8 before the infected cell is at the center of that row sets up a single-piece finish that clears the infection; reversing order (filling the same cells but starting from the far end) may trigger spread before the clear is possible.

## Candidate L2-2 — Siege

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - A "wall" of filled sentinel cells (`'#b04000'`) occupies the entire bottom row at round start (or a randomly generated partial bottom row for higher difficulty — generator forward-sims to guarantee the wall can be cleared).
  - After every placement that does NOT clear the wall row, the wall advances one row upward: every sentinel cell shifts from row R to row R-1. Empty cells in the wall row do not advance.
  - Clearing the wall row (any row clear that includes all sentinel cells in that row) destroys those sentinels and resets the advance counter.
  - The wall can advance a maximum of N rows (N = difficulty parameter). If it reaches row 0 (the top row) the round is lost.
  - A new wall segment regenerates at the bottom row 2 turns after the previous one is cleared (generator guarantees pieces exist to clear it again).
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND no wall segment currently active (last wall segment cleared and regeneration timer has not yet spawned a new one within the piece budget).
- Lose condition: wall row reaches row 0 (top of board).
- New question per placement: "Does placing here contribute to clearing the current wall row this turn, or does it leave the wall row at 7/8 full — meaning the wall advances before I can finish it next placement?" The wall's upward march sets a deadline; each piece either advances the clear or buys the wall a row.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: filling non-wall rows to score intermediate clears also consumes pieces that might have been used to complete the wall row clear; the same piece count does double or single duty depending on targeting.
- Antagonist: the wall — it occupies the bottom row as sentinel cells, advances toward the top each move it survives, and is defeated by a line clear that empties its entire row.
- Negative-space constraint: cells in the wall row that are NOT yet filled by the wall are not "free" — they must be reached by piece placement to complete the clear, so the player cannot ignore them as neutral empty space.
- Order coupling: placing pieces that fill non-wall rows first uses up piece budget before the wall is cleared; placing wall-row pieces first clears the wall but leaves later rows harder to fill, since no pieces remain for them. Piece sequence determines whether the wall survives one extra advance tick.

## Candidate L2-3 — Fuse

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - Board is pre-seeded with B "bomb" cells (color `'#e63c00'`). Each bomb carries a counter displayed inside it (e.g. 3, 2, 1).
  - After each placement, each bomb's counter decrements by 1.
  - When a bomb's counter reaches 0, it detonates: it fills its own cell plus all 4 orthogonal neighbors with non-clearable "rubble" cells (color `'#4a4a4a'`) that can never be part of a line clear (they block clearing of any row/column they occupy).
  - A bomb is DEFEATED by clearing the row or column that contains it before its counter reaches 0. When that row/column is cleared, the bomb is removed instead of detonating.
  - Generator seeds bombs with counters such that the known solution path clears each bomb's row/column exactly 1 turn before detonation (solvability via forward-sim).
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND all bombs cleared (none detonated) AND no rubble cells on the board.
- Lose condition: any bomb detonates (creates rubble), OR any rubble cell exists at round end preventing the win check from being satisfied, OR no valid placement remains (classic game-over).
- New question per placement: "Does placing here advance the clear on bomb B's row in time to clear it before its fuse hits 0, or does it consume a piece needed for that row while also decrementing B's counter?" Every placement both burns a unit of the fuse AND may or may not contribute to extinguishing it — the same action progresses both the threat and potentially its defeat.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: each piece placement simultaneously decrements every bomb (advancing the threat) and fills cells (potentially completing the clear that defeats a bomb). The same act of placing is both the threat accelerant and the threat solution.
- Antagonist: bomb cells — they occupy cells with a visible countdown, detonate if not cleared in time, and are neutralized by the same line-clear mechanic used for scoring.
- Negative-space constraint: cells in a bomb's row/column that the player leaves empty to place other pieces delay the clear, each empty cell is a ticking cost.
- Order coupling: placing the piece that completes bomb B's row on move 3 vs. move 5 (out of 6 total pieces) changes which bombs can still be saved; the same set of pieces placed in different order leaves different bombs detonated.

## Candidate L2-4 — Anchor

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - One cell on the board is designated the "anchor" (color `'#1a3a8f'`). It does not move.
  - Each occupied cell on the board (not the anchor itself) that is NOT 4-connected (directly or transitively) to the anchor cell contributes to a "drift count."
  - After each placement, any filled cell that is not connected to the anchor is swept: it is converted to a new "debris" cell (color `'#6b6b6b'`) in the same position. Debris cells cannot be part of line clears and cannot be placed over.
  - A cell becomes connected to the anchor if there is a path of filled cells (in the 4-directions) from it to the anchor's row/column.
  - Debris cells are DEFEATED by placing a piece that — together with existing fills — creates a line that includes that debris cell's row or column; but debris blocks the clear (standard rule: debris counts as filled but the line must be otherwise fully filled with normal cells). Wait — revised: debris cells are PERMANENT and cannot be cleared; the only way to avoid debris is to place all pieces while maintaining connectivity to the anchor.
  - Generator forward-sims to guarantee connectivity can be maintained with the given piece set.
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND zero debris cells on the board (every filled cell was placed while connected to the anchor).
- Lose condition: any debris cell is created at any point (strict mode) OR debris cell count exceeds D at round end.
- New question per placement: "Does placing this piece in this position maintain 4-connectivity of all filled cells to the anchor, or does it create an isolated island that immediately becomes permanent debris?" Connectivity is a geometric planning question that changes with every placement since the anchor's neighborhood changes as pieces accumulate.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: placing pieces away from the anchor region scores future line-clears (they fill lines far from the anchor) but risks connectivity — the same distant placement that would complete a line may also orphan the cells between it and the anchor.
- Antagonist: anchor cell — it constrains legal placement geography: any fill that breaks 4-connectivity to the anchor self-destructs into permanent debris. Defeat condition = maintain zero debris across all placements.
- Negative-space constraint: cells between the anchor and the intended placement location cannot be left empty if they are the only path of connectivity; negative space along the connectivity route is forbidden.
- Order coupling: placing a "bridge" piece (a long 1×4 connecting two regions) early maintains routes that let later pieces be placed freely anywhere; placing far pieces first orphans them unless the bridge piece happens to come later.

## Candidate L2-5 — Flood

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - The board has a pre-defined "flood source" cell in one corner (top-left, row 0 col 0) marked with a distinct color (`'#0055cc'`).
  - At the start of the round, the flood source is active.
  - After each placement, flood expands: all empty cells 4-adjacent to any current flood cell become flood cells (BFS one step outward per placement).
  - Flood cells are empty cells that have been "claimed" by the flood; they are rendered with a blue tint but count as empty (pieces can still be placed on them, which converts them to piece color and removes the flood marker).
  - A flood cell is DEFEATED (removed) when a piece is placed on it, converting it to a normal piece cell.
  - Flood is fully defeated when zero flood cells remain (all have been covered by pieces or blocked by filled cells).
  - If flood reaches the opposite corner cell (row 7, col 7) the round is lost.
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND zero flood cells remain on the board AND the opposite corner cell is not a flood cell.
- Lose condition: flood cell reaches row 7, col 7 at any point.
- New question per placement: "Does placing here block the flood's BFS expansion path toward the far corner, OR does it expose a new flood-expansion route by clearing a row that was previously blocking the flood?" Line clears eliminate piece cells that were acting as flood barriers, potentially opening new flood paths — the clear that scores points may also uncork a new flood route.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: line clears remove pieces (good for score/space) but also remove cells that were physically blocking flood expansion (bad, as those cells were a free flood barrier).
- Antagonist: flood cells — they expand BFS one step per placement, are removed by placing pieces on them, and lose the round if they reach the far corner.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells adjacent to the flood's current boundary are constrained — they will become flood cells next turn if not filled, creating a soft forced-move pressure.
- Order coupling: placing a long horizontal piece early creates a flood barrier that blocks several expansion paths simultaneously; placing small pieces first allows flood to spread into the blocked region before the barrier arrives, requiring more total cells to contain.

## Candidate L2-6 — Rival

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - Board is pre-filled with two distinct pre-fill colors: player's "own" color cells (target cells to keep, same as Puzzle mode) and "rival" cells (color `'#c0392b'`).
  - Rival cells occupy a contiguous region. After every player placement, the rival region GROWS by 1 cell: the empty cell with the highest "pressure" (most rival-adjacent empty neighbors) becomes a new rival cell.
  - Rival cells are DEFEATED by line clears: any rival cell in a cleared row or column is permanently removed.
  - The player's own target cells, if cleared, are also removed — they must be retained for the win condition.
  - Generator ensures: (a) the rival region is defeatable with the given pieces via forward-sim, and (b) the rival never physically reaches the player's target cells before it can be cleared (solvability guarantee).
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND zero rival cells remain AND all target cells still on the board (not cleared).
- Lose condition: rival region reaches size S (a per-difficulty ceiling) OR a rival cell occupies a target cell position (overwrites it).
- New question per placement: "Does placing here trigger a line clear that removes rival cells, or does it grow the board density in a region the rival will expand into next turn, making the expansion cell even harder to clear later?" The rival's growth target (highest-pressure empty cell) changes each turn based on the player's placements, so the same piece placed in different positions changes which rival cell will be hardest to clear next.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: filling cells adjacent to rival region builds toward clears (want) but also increases adjacency pressure on empty cells near the rival, potentially directing rival growth into areas the player needs to keep empty for future piece placement (fear).
- Antagonist: rival cells — a growing colored region that expands one cell per turn, is removed by line clears, and loses the round if it reaches size S or touches a target cell.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells adjacent to the rival's boundary are not neutral — their emptiness determines where the rival expands; the player may deliberately leave cells empty to "steer" rival growth away from target cells.
- Order coupling: clearing the rival early (using pieces to complete rival-containing rows first) reduces its size before it can grow large; clearing late means the rival region has expanded into harder-to-clear positions.

## Candidate L2-7 — Chain

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - Board is pre-seeded with C "chain links" (color `'#7b3f00'`). Each chain link is 4-connected to at least one adjacent chain link (they form a contiguous chain path from one board edge to another — like a dividing line across the board).
  - A chain link is DEFEATED (removed) when its row or column is cleared.
  - Chain links block placements: they are treated as filled cells (pieces cannot be placed on them).
  - If the chain is not fully defeated (all chain link cells cleared) by the time the last piece is placed, the round is lost.
  - Generator guarantees: the chain path is such that clearing a subset of rows/columns that includes all chain link cells is achievable with the given piece set (forward-sim proof).
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND all chain link cells have been cleared (zero chain links remain on the board).
- Lose condition: any chain link remains after the last piece is placed.
- New question per placement: "Does placing here contribute to clearing the row that contains the next chain link I need to remove — or does it fill a non-chain row that will make that chain row harder to complete later?" Since chain links span the board and block placements in their cells, route planning through the chain is a distinct geometric problem from pure score maximization.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: line clears are the only verb for removing chain links AND for score, but each clear consumes the pieces used to fill that row/column — pieces spent on a non-chain-row are pieces not spent on chain rows.
- Antagonist: chain links — a connected path of filled sentinel cells spanning the board that must be cleared row-by-row or column-by-column, blocking normal piece placement in their positions.
- Negative-space constraint: cells NOT occupied by the chain but in the same rows/columns as chain links must be filled by pieces to complete those clears; the player cannot skip these empty cells.
- Order coupling: clearing chain rows from one end of the chain to the other (sequential order) uses fewer total pieces than clearing them in random order because each sequential clear shortens the chain by the maximum number of links; alternating order may require two separate clears of the same row (if the chain doubles back).

## Candidate L2-8 — Tide

- Lens: 2 (add antagonist)
- Generated by: Claude Sonnet
- Rule changes:
  - The bottom two rows of the board are the "tide zone." At round start they are empty.
  - After each player placement, one random cell in the tide zone that is currently empty gets filled by a tide cell (color `'#006080'`). The random cell is chosen via seeded deterministic RNG (seed = puzzle ID + move index), so behavior is fully learnable on replay.
  - A tide cell is DEFEATED by being part of a completed row clear — when the row containing it is fully filled and cleared, the tide cell is removed along with all other cells in that row.
  - If any tide cell remains at round end AND any tide-zone row has no piece cells in it at all (i.e. the tide filled the row entirely by itself), the round is lost (the tide drowned the board).
  - Generator forward-sims from the fixed RNG sequence to guarantee the tide pattern is clearable with the given pieces.
- Win condition: all tray pieces placed AND zero tide cells remain in the tide zone.
- Lose condition: any tide-zone row is fully occupied by tide cells alone (player placed zero pieces in that row) at any point, creating an "unclearable" tide row (tide cells are not pieces, so a row of only tide cells cannot trigger a clear).
- New question per placement: "Do I place in the tide zone now to block the tide from filling that row, or do I place elsewhere and risk the tide claiming a tide-zone row entirely?" The RNG sequence is fixed and visible (shown as a small indicator of which tide-zone cells will fill next), so the question is about acting on known information vs. acting on board geometry elsewhere.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: placing pieces in the tide zone defeats the tide (want) but also pre-commits piece cells to low-value rows (fear — those pieces could have cleared higher-scoring upper rows). The same piece fills either a tide-threatened cell or a score-maximizing upper row.
- Antagonist: tide cells — deterministic-RNG-seeded cells that fill the tide zone one per turn, and are removed only by line clears of their row; they drown the round if they fill a tide-zone row uncontested.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells in the tide zone are constrained because leaving them empty invites tide fill; the player cannot freely ignore the bottom rows even if upper-row scoring is more efficient.
- Order coupling: placing pieces in the tide zone early (first 2-3 placements) keeps the tide zone controlled but depletes pieces before upper rows are built; placing tide-zone pieces late means the tide may have already filled those rows, requiring different (now more constrained) piece routing.
