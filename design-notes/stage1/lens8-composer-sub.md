## Candidate L8-1 — Erase (piece subtraction: inverted placement)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: Piece placement normally ADDS cells to the board; here, piece placement SUBTRACTS the piece's footprint from existing filled cells.
- Rule changes:
  - Board is pre-filled with a solid pattern (e.g., 64/64 cells filled, or a large connected shape).
  - A piece may only be placed by its footprint ENTIRELY overlapping filled cells; placement erases those cells.
  - Empty cells cannot be placed on; placement must fully land on filled cells.
  - No line clears. No tray refill on empty (tray has finite pieces: e.g., 9 pieces total).
  - Goal: erase the pre-fill pattern into a target shape (or to zero cells).
- Win condition: board state matches exact target pattern (specific empty-cell set within pre-fill), OR all cells erased if target is empty.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted with board ≠ target, OR no piece in current tray has a legal placement (all footprints would overhang empty space or the void).
- New question per placement: "Which filled region should I erase with this piece to preserve a future placement footprint for the pieces still in my tray?" Every placement is a destructive choice; empty space is the goal, not a penalty.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece footprint. (1) Wanted: the piece's shape precisely matches the filled region you want to erase. (2) Feared: the piece's shape is slightly misaligned, leaving isolated filled cells that no future piece can land on (dead filled zones).
- Antagonist: the pre-fill pattern geometry itself — isolated islands of fill that are the wrong shape for remaining tray pieces become traps.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells created by early erasures must align with the footprints of later pieces; a L-shaped hole left too early may not fit the upcoming T-piece, forcing a loss.
- Order coupling: erasing the left half first leaves the right half isolated if pieces are not carefully sequenced; same 9 pieces in different order produce different reachable target patterns.
- Inversion-coherence check: PASS. Subtraction is the opposite of addition; the inversion is semantically clear. Placement must match filled cells only (new rule with consequence). Tray finiteness guarantees solvability via generator forward-sim. The mechanic is meaningful: no random punishment, high coupling to order and geometry.

## Candidate L8-2 — Void King (game-over inversion: too many legal moves)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: Game-over normally means NO valid moves remain; here, game-over means TOO MANY valid moves remain (player loses the ability to choose strategically).
- Rule changes:
  - Each piece in the tray is placed with a mandatory "lock window": once placed, that piece is locked in 2 turns (wall-clock turns, not placements; e.g., piece placed on turn 1 is locked on turn 3).
  - At any point, if the number of CURRENTLY UNLOCKED pieces with legal placements on the board exceeds a threshold T (e.g., T=4), the player must immediately choose one to place; if they cannot or refuse, they lose.
  - Pre-fill encodes a target pattern. Goal: end state matches target AND all pieces locked/placed.
  - A locked piece cannot be moved or replaced; it is frozen.
- Win condition: all pieces placed, all pieces locked, board = target.
- Lose condition: at the start of a turn, unlocked-piece-count > T AND the player has not immediately placed one piece in the previous action.
- New question per placement: "I have 3 unlocked pieces with legal placements. Placing this 4th unlocks the 5th, which has only 1 legal position. If I place the 4th now, the 5th locks me into a forced choice. Should I lock myself in now or wait?" The decision tree becomes a timing puzzle where fewer options (pieces locked) is strategically desirable.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece lock-window. (1) Wanted: a locked piece is one fewer choice on the next turn, reducing decision tree size. (2) Feared: locking a piece early limits future placements for remaining pieces if the lock-window expires before ideal geometry is reachable.
- Antagonist: the decision-space explosion itself — too many free pieces with legal moves forces a loss. The antagonist is internal: the player's own geometric optionality.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells you leave for "later" must remain empty long enough that the pieces locking into that space do so during their legal window.
- Order coupling: placing pieces in sequence that keeps unlocked-count ≤ T requires careful turn-by-turn ordering. Same pieces, different order, different lock-expiration timelines.
- Inversion-coherence check: FAIL (AUTO-REJECT). The mechanic is inversion-clever, but game-over condition feels like a side-effect, not a structural consequence of inverting a primitive. The "too many moves" rule is imposed, not derived from inverting a semantic primitive. The mode conflates two ideas: lock-windows (interesting) and "game over if too many choices" (mechanical, not inversion-derived). Reframe needed.

## Candidate L8-3 — Hoard (tray refill inversion: tray depletes, pieces regenerate from clears)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: Tray refills on empty; here, tray DEPLETES and the player must GENERATE NEW PIECES via line clears (clears refund pieces to the tray, not clear-rewards to a score).
- Rule changes:
  - Tray starts with 3 pieces (as Classic). No refill on empty.
  - When a row or column clears, instead of awarding points, the clear generates 1 NEW PIECE (random shape from a fixed set, or seeded for solvability) and adds it to the tray.
  - The generated piece is placed into the tray, not immediately on the board.
  - Pre-fill present (Puzzle-style target pattern).
  - Goal: clear all pre-fill.
- Win condition: all pre-fill cells emptied via line clears.
- Lose condition: tray exhausted (0 pieces remain) AND board still has pre-fill unfilled (cannot place, cannot generate new pieces).
- New question per placement: "Should I clear row 3 now (which generates a random 4-cell piece I might not need) or place my current piece to set up a larger multi-row clear later (which generates a better-shaped piece for the endgame)?" Clears are no longer a reward; they are the ONLY lever for tray generation.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Line clears. (1) Wanted: clears delete pre-fill and generate new pieces to continue play. (2) Feared: a clear might generate a useless shape (e.g., a 4-cell I when you need a 2-cell pair), leaving the new tray worse, forcing a reset-harder puzzle.
- Antagonist: pre-fill (must be cleared) AND piece scarcity (if you clear suboptimally, the tray weakens).
- Negative-space constraint: cells left empty for "future clear" must be reachable by tray pieces; if you burn all pieces on early sub-optimal clears, the tray refills with pieces that don't fit remaining gaps.
- Order coupling: clearing rows in sequence (row 3, then row 7) generates pieces in that order, affecting what can be placed next. Reverse order, different pieces, different endgame state.
- Inversion-coherence check: PASS. Refill is inverted: tray depletes, but clears regenerate pieces. The inversion creates a meaningful dual-purpose: clears are no longer pure reward, they are the ONLY way to extend play. The mechanic is high-coupling (clear choice affects future tray composition). Risk: generator must prove solvability (piece RNG must be seeded or constrained); fair complexity for forward-sim.

## Candidate L8-4 — Unstack (line clear inversion: lines FILL, not clear)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: Line clears normally DELETE rows/columns when full; here, line FILLS: when a row or column is completely empty, it automatically FILLS with a non-clearable "fill" cell (color `'#999999'`), blocking that region permanently.
- Rule changes:
  - When a row or column becomes completely empty (all 8 cells are empty), all cells in that row/column are immediately filled with non-clearable "fill" cells.
  - Fill cells cannot be placed over, cannot be part of a future line clear, cannot be erased.
  - Pre-fill present (Puzzle target).
  - Goal: clear all pre-fill.
- Win condition: all pre-fill cells emptied via line clears, AND all rows/columns still have at least 1 empty cell (to prevent auto-fill).
- Lose condition: a row/column becomes completely empty and auto-fills OR tray exhausted with pre-fill remaining.
- New question per placement: "If I place here, row 5 becomes 7/8 full. If I don't place there, I leave row 5 with 0/8, which auto-fills with non-clearable cells. Do I commit to filling row 5 or accept its loss?" Every empty row/col is a ticking timer: fill it before it empties, or lose it.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Empty cells / line fills. (1) Wanted: leaving a row at 0/8 to auto-fill might clear a pre-fill cell that blocked a clearing line. (2) Feared: auto-fill converts a once-useful row into a permanent dead region, narrowing future placement options.
- Antagonist: the auto-fill trap — rows/columns the player did not plan to fill, but cannot prevent from being auto-filled if they reach 0/8.
- Negative-space constraint: cells the player leaves empty must not align into complete rows/columns, or the auto-fill triggers. Negative space management is inverted: empty regions are now dangerous.
- Order coupling: placing piece A first fills row 4 to 1/8, preventing auto-fill; placing piece B first fills column 2 to 3/8 from a different angle. Piece order changes which rows/columns are at risk.
- Inversion-coherence check: FAIL (AUTO-REJECT). The inversion is cosmetic. "Line fills" instead of "line clears" is a name-flip, but the mechanic is still "do X to clear a line." The auto-fill rule is bolted on, not derived. Testing anti-patterns: the fill rule is a function of "row became empty" (which is automatic given placement choices), so it smells like Mirror disease. Reframe needed: what if line-fills were the PRIMARY verb, not a punishment?

## Candidate L8-5 — Inward Spiral (board edge inversion: board EMPTIES toward center, not fills toward edge)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: Board normally fills from placement until the board is full (lose). Here, board EMPTIES inward: the outer edges of the board are "erosion zones" that automatically clear over time; pieces placed near the edge are at risk of erosion.
- Rule changes:
  - Board has a "ring distance" for each cell: distance 0 is the center (rows 2-5, cols 2-5), distance 1 is the next ring outward, distance 2 is the edge (rows 0-1 and 6-7, cols 0-1 and 6-7).
  - After each placement, all filled cells at ring distance = 2 (edges) age by 1 turn. After 3 turns, edge cells are automatically erased.
  - Cells at distance 1 age by 1 every other turn. Cells at distance 0 never age.
  - Pre-fill present (target pattern, centrally located).
  - Goal: clear pre-fill by placing pieces such that all eroded cells leave the pre-fill intact.
- Win condition: all pre-fill cells are empty.
- Lose condition: pre-fill cells erode (aged off the board), OR tray exhausted with pre-fill remaining.
- New question per placement: "If I place this piece at the edge to fill row 7, the edge fills now; it will age out in 2 more turns. Should I avoid the edge and place centrally instead, even if the central placement is sub-optimal for clearing pre-fill?" Placement location is now a time-cost decision.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece placement location (distance from center). (1) Wanted: edge placement opens space for pre-fill clearing if pre-fill is near the edge. (2) Feared: edge cells age, forcing a re-clear or a placement re-do if the eroded cells were meant to support a future clear.
- Antagonist: erosion (cells age from edges inward). The antagonist is both external (erosion happens automatically) and internal (player's own edge-placed pieces become liabilities).
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells at the edge that are meant to be preserved for future clears age away if left too long; the window to place a piece on an edge-empty-cell before erosion narrows it is limited.
- Order coupling: placing an edge piece in tray 1 vs. tray 3 gives it 0 vs. 2 aging turns before it erodes. Same piece shape, different tray sequence, different erosion pressure.
- Inversion-coherence check: FAIL (AUTO-REJECT). "Board empties" is not a clear inversion of an existing primitive; it's a new environmental rule (erosion) grafted onto placement. The inversion is not structural — there is no primitive being inverted. The mechanic is interesting (age+erase) but does not satisfy the Lens 8 criterion of flipping a semantic primitive.

## Candidate L8-6 — Stamp (piece occupancy inversion: piece IS the empty cells, not the filled cells)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: A piece normally occupies its cells by FILLING them; here, a piece occupies its cells by MARKING them EMPTY (the piece's footprint is the empty region, not the filled region).
- Rule changes:
  - When a piece is placed, instead of filling cells, the piece's footprint is marked as EMPTY (or "reserved empty"). These cells are now off-limits for future placements (they are reserved for the piece, but the piece itself does not occupy space visually).
  - The board has a pre-fill target pattern. Pieces are placed by drawing the "negative" pattern: places where the piece goes are left empty in the target.
  - Example: if the target is a filled 8x8 board with a 3x3 empty hole in the center, placing a 3x3 piece at the center reserves that hole (the piece "is" the hole).
  - Goal: reserve all empty regions of the target pattern.
- Win condition: every empty cell in the target pattern has been reserved by a placed piece footprint (no overlapping piece footprints).
- Lose condition: tray exhausted with unreserved empty cells in the target, OR a piece footprint would overlap a previously placed piece footprint (illegal placement).
- New question per placement: "Which empty region in the target should this piece footprint reserve, and does the shape of the piece exactly match the shape of the empty region?" Pieces are no longer space-fillers; they are empty-space-claimers.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece shape (footprint). (1) Wanted: the piece's shape matches an empty region in the target, reserving it before another piece claims it. (2) Feared: the piece's shape is slightly too large or rotated wrong, and it overlaps another piece's reserved region, forcing a loss.
- Antagonist: the target's empty-region geometry — mismatched piece shapes that cannot exactly tile the empty regions.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells not part of the target pattern must remain empty (not reserved by pieces); pieces cannot overhang outside the target's intended empty regions.
- Order coupling: reserving the center hole first allows the corner holes to be reserved by smaller pieces; reserving the corners first may force the center piece to be the large one left, which may not fit the center hole. Piece order changes which empty regions are still reachable.
- Inversion-coherence check: PASS. Piece occupancy is inverted: pieces ARE the empty cells, not the filled cells. The mechanic is semantically clear and flips the puzzle's perspective: instead of filling a target, you're emptying one. Solvability: generator forward-sims to ensure all empty regions can be tiled. Dual-purpose: piece shape is wanted (matches empty) and feared (doesn't match, forcing a loss). Order coupling: high. NEW QUESTION: fundamentally different from base — "which empty region?" instead of "which filled region?" Anti-pattern check: none detected.

## Candidate L8-7 — Invert Score (reward inversion: score increases for AVOIDING clears, not making them)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: Score normally increases when lines clear (reward); here, score increases for AVOIDING clears (each placement that does NOT trigger a clear awards +1 point).
- Rule changes:
  - No line clears occur (clears are disabled).
  - Each placement that fits on the board (without overlap or overflow) awards +1 point.
  - Pre-fill present (target pattern: a specific set of empty cells that must remain empty).
  - Goal: reach score threshold S (e.g., S=20 points = 20 safe placements) while the target empty cells stay empty.
- Win condition: score >= S AND all target empty cells remain empty at the end of the tray.
- Lose condition: a placement overlaps a target empty cell (loses immediately), OR score < S at tray exhaustion.
- New question per placement: "Can I place this piece without touching the target empty cells? And does this placement leave the board in a state where the next piece can also avoid the target?" Avoidance becomes the strategic core; filling the board WITHOUT touching forbidden cells is the puzzle.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece placement. (1) Wanted: placements score and advance toward the goal. (2) Feared: scoring a placement uses up board space, narrowing options for remaining placements to also avoid target cells.
- Antagonist: board fullness (as pieces accumulate, the board fills) AND the target empty-cell constraints (player must navigate around them).
- Negative-space constraint: target empty cells are "walls" that divide the board into regions; pieces must fit within regions that can sustain all remaining pieces without colliding with target cells.
- Order coupling: placing a wide piece first (an I-tetromino) in the left half uses up the left region; later pieces must fit the right half. Reverse order, different space partitioning, different final board state.
- Inversion-coherence check: FAIL (AUTO-REJECT). Score as a reward is not a "primitive" in the sense of the Lens 8 spec (primitives are mechanics: placement, clears, color, rotation, board, tray, piece). Inverting a scoring rule (reward to penalty or vice versa) is too shallow; it's a metagame knob, not a semantic primitive. The mode itself (avoid clears, hit a target) is interesting, but it's not an inversion-via-Lens-8. Reframe: what IS the primitive here? Clears are disabled entirely, so the inversion would be "clears disable themselves" or "clears are not possible" — that's removal, not inversion.

## Candidate L8-8 — Asymmetric Mirror (placement inversion: pieces place BACKWARD through a mirror axis)

- Lens: 8 (inversion)
- Generated by: Claude Haiku (Composer substitute)
- What's inverted: Piece placement normally writes the piece to the board in its dealt orientation; here, piece placement writes the piece MIRRORED across a board axis (vertical, horizontal, or diagonal).
- Rule changes:
  - Board has a designated mirror line (e.g., vertical axis at column 3.5, dividing left and right halves).
  - When a piece is placed at position P on the left half, it is ALSO automatically placed at the mirrored position P' on the right half, in the mirrored orientation.
  - Pre-fill may exist asymmetrically on one side.
  - Pieces are placed one on the left; the mirror placement is automatic (no additional action).
  - Goal: match a target pattern (which may be asymmetric or symmetric).
- Win condition: board state equals target pattern (including the mirrored version).
- Lose condition: either placement position has overlap OR a placement would overflow the board OR tray exhausted with board ≠ target.
- New question per placement: "If I place this L-piece on the left at row 3, col 2, it mirrors to row 3, col 5 on the right. Does the mirrored version collide with pre-fill or previously mirrored pieces?" Placement must check both the direct and mirrored positions.
- Dual-purpose mechanic: Piece placement. (1) Wanted: the mirrored placement automatically fills symmetric regions, reducing the number of placements needed. (2) Feared: a mirrored placement collides on the opposite side, making the direct placement illegal.
- Antagonist: the mirror constraint — asymmetric pre-fill or target geometry that forces the player to place pieces such that the mirror side remains unconstrained.
- Negative-space constraint: empty cells on one side must have corresponding empty cells on the mirror side; if you leave the left side with a gap, the mirrored gap must also be unoccupied.
- Order coupling: placing piece A first fills the left side and mirrors it to the right; the mirrored state then constrains where piece B can go on the left. Piece order determines which sides are filled first, affecting future placements.
- Inversion-coherence check: FAIL (AUTO-REJECT). Placement does not invert; the rule added is AUTOMATIC MIRRORING, not an inverted placement primitive. Pieces still place normally on the left; the right side is a side-effect. This is closer to "add a rule" (Lens 2) than "invert a primitive" (Lens 8). The inversion of "piece placement" would be something like "piece placement REMOVES cells" (see L8-1, Erase), not "piece placement is accompanied by a mirror."
