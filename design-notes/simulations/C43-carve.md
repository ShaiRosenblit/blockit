# C43 — Carve: Stage 3 reducer simulation

## Rules recap (mechanics-speak)

- Board starts (near) fully filled with pre-fill cells.
- Each placement of a tray piece *erases* (sets to null) the cells under its footprint, instead of writing.
- Placement legality: every footprint cell must currently be FILLED (mirror of Classic's "must be empty").
- After carving, if any row OR column is now fully empty, that row/col is "tide-refilled" — re-written with pre-fill cells, undoing the player's carving on those lanes.
- Win condition: the empty pattern matches a target negative.

## Legend

```
#   pre-fill (the substance to carve)
.   empty (carved or originally empty)
T   target-empty cell (must end empty for win)
*   target-filled cell (must end filled — i.e. must NOT be carved)
```

For board states I'll show actual fill, with target overlay implied separately.

## Verdict

**Question persists?** NO. The "which cells do I carve" question collapses early into a binary feasibility check: either a tray piece's footprint fits exactly within remaining-T-cells-currently-filled, or it doesn't. There's no gradient of choice — there's "legal and target-respecting" vs "illegal-or-loses." The question becomes "find ANY footprint that doesn't over-carve."

**Trade-off space alive?** NO. The trace converged immediately to "match piece footprints to remaining T pockets." If a piece doesn't fit a T pocket, you over-carve and lose. There's no second viable strategy — there's just feasibility check.

**Difficulty curve plausible?** NO. The puzzle's difficulty is entirely front-loaded into the generator's choice of (target, tray): if the generator produces a (target, tray) pair where every piece has a valid all-T footprint at every step, the puzzle is *trivial* (just place each piece on its obvious spot). If the generator produces an unsolvable pair, the puzzle is impossible. There's no middle ground where the player makes interesting trade-offs.

**The tide mechanic is broken in this design.** Tide refills an *entire* row/col, including T cells you correctly carved. So tide is never a tool — it's only a punishment. The "dual-purpose mechanic" claim (tide both wanted and feared) fails: tide is exclusively feared. This is exactly the risk the candidate flagged in its "Immediate suspicion" section ("if tide rule never fires the mode collapses to constraint puzzle, if it does fire it's catastrophic").

**Additional structural problem revealed by simulation:** Carving pieces can ONLY land on currently-filled cells. Once you carve a region, that region is permanently unavailable as a footprint host. So pieces must be applied to *intact* regions, and the "intact filled regions" shrink monotonically. If your tray has a 2×2 piece and your target needs only single-cell empties scattered through filled regions, you cannot use the 2×2 without over-carving. The constraint "footprint must be fully filled" means piece geometry must exactly match remaining-target-pocket geometry — turning the puzzle into a packing test, not a reasoning game.

**Compare to Puzzle mode:** Puzzle's pieces add cells; you can place a piece partially over already-filled cells? No — Classic also requires all-empty. But Puzzle's RECOVERY mechanism is line clears — fill a row, get the cells back as empty space. Carve has no analog: there's no way to *re-fill* over-carved cells without triggering a tide that wipes the line.

**The real issue:** Carving is the inverse of placement, but the recovery primitive (line clear in Classic = tide in Carve) is also inverted, and the inversion makes recovery destructive instead of constructive. The mechanic doesn't have a recovery loop, only a punishment loop.

**Verdict: REJECT.**

Structural collapse confirmed:
1. Dominant strategy is deterministic (footprint-matches-T-pocket lookup).
2. Tide is a one-way punishment, not a tool.
3. Solvability is determined entirely at generation time; player input is forced or losing.
4. No second viable strategy emerges in trace.

This is a constraint-satisfaction puzzle in disguise, not a placement game.
